# THE STATIC CLEARS — a cleaner picture, a louder crowd, and the show finally adjusts to you

*Standards and Practices sends its regrets. It has none.*

## The picture got cleaner

Every screen used to run through an old-TV filter — darkened corners, scan
lines, a curved-monitor frame, the works. It's gone. Nothing between you and
the set now but the set. If you liked the grime, you're out of luck; the
network's insurers weren't fans either.

## The crowd sounds like a crowd now

The old cheer was built from filtered noise — technically a roar, honestly a
hiss. It's been rebuilt from the ground up as real, pitched voices: dozens of
overlapping shouts with a falling pitch glide, tuned against an actual
recorded crowd until the frequencies matched. It's a wall of noise now, not a
wall of static.

And the crowd doesn't wait for the end of the room anymore — it pops at the
end of **every wave**, so the room doesn't go quiet between fights. The
countdown gets its own moment too: the instant it hits zero, the booth calls
**"HERE. WE. GO!!"** and the horde is already coming.

## Something for wandering hands

Sponsors occasionally leave fan mail lying around the set — a stray envelope
tucked somewhere in the room, never blocking a fight, never required. Grab it
and the announcer reads a line from it; skip it and nothing changes except
the tally on your score screen at the end of the night. Purely for the
completionists.

## The show adjusts to you

A few firsts for SLASH TV:

- **The pacing bends if you're struggling.** Die twice in the same room in
  one sitting and the next attempt spaces its spawns out a little —
  capped at 30% slower, and only the pacing moves. Same zombies, same
  numbers, same fight — just a little less relentless.
- **A flawless run gets its own ending.** Clear all three episodes without
  a single contestant ever going down, and the final broadcast signs off
  differently. Standards and Practices has no idea what to file it under.
- **Two new ways to be recognised at the end of a run**: 🎭 **SOLO ACT**
  for going in with no partner and no revive safety net, and 🎬 **SAVED BY
  THE BELL** for going down mid-run and still walking out a winner.
- **Endless Arena is open to everyone now**, from the first time you hit the
  title screen — it used to be locked behind beating Episode 1, which meant
  the one place built for practice was hidden from the people who needed it
  most.

## Built for the couch, fixed for the phone

- The **pause button** was too small and sat right on top of the score
  counter. It's bigger now, with real space around it.
- **Menu text and buttons** across the board are larger and bolder on
  touch devices — they were sized for a desktop screen before.
- **iPhone can't do real fullscreen** — that's an iOS limitation, not a bug
  — so there's now a one-time tip pointing you at Add to Home Screen
  instead, which gets you the closest thing iOS allows.
- The fullscreen button moved out from under the pause button so the two
  stop fighting for the same corner.

## Also in this broadcast

- Pressing fire now skips straight through the "walking on set" intro
  instead of forcing a blind wait on every single retry.
- Chromatic aberration and a freeze-frame beat on big kills, from a few
  updates back and still live: spitters and gushers visibly wind up before
  they fire, so the shot is always telegraphed, never a surprise.
